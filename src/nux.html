Context

Ten-minute delivery runs on riders who stay

Blinkit’s delivery promise depends on having enough riders online near each store. Before riders take their first order, they’ve already been verified and trained. The first week is where that investment can turn into a lasting working relationship.

  1. ✓OnboardingGet verified and ready to ride.
  2. First deliveryStart taking orders and earning.
  3. ↗First 50 deliveriesDecide whether to keep coming back.
~60% of new riders didn’t reach 50 deliveries within seven days.

The problem

The money was there. Riders couldn’t see it.

When we spoke to riders who had stopped delivering, many described a gap between what they expected to earn and what they saw on the payout page. That page showed order pay. Incentives lived separately, and multiple offers had conditions riders had to understand from day one.

  1. The full picture was one tap away. Riders saw order pay upfront, but had to look elsewhere for incentives.
  2. There were too many offers to track. With several offers running at once, it was hard to know which one to work toward.
~58% of interviewed riders who stopped delivering cited a gap between expected and actual earnings.

The bet

Before new screens, a pilot

For the first seven days after a rider’s first delivery, the pilot ran two offers and removed the extra eligibility conditions. The configuration change let the team test a simpler offer experience before investing in new screens.

03 / Proposed design

One next goal. More detail when it matters.

A staged offer journey gives riders one thing to aim for early, explains new conditions as they appear, and brings order pay and unlocked incentives into the same view.

  1. First 20 ordersOne offer, no extra eligibility conditions.
  2. 20–50 ordersOne offer with visible milestones and a guided introduction to eligibility.
  3. Later in the journeyTransition to the standard store offers.

01 / First 20 orders

Make the next milestone obvious.

Show one daily offer and the next milestone on the main screen. Keep the remaining milestones in the offer details, where riders can check what’s ahead without crowding the immediate decision.

02 / 20–50 orders

Explain the new rule when it appears.

Introduce the first lock-in condition through a coachmark at the moment it becomes relevant. Show all milestones together so riders can plan around the requirements.

03 / Earnings

Show what riders actually earned.

Put total earnings first, with order pay and unlocked incentives side by side in the breakdown. Keep trips, login hours, and potential earnings per hour in a secondary view, with a clear distinction between earned and potential amounts.

04 / What to measure

Will riders understand offers as the rules get more complex?

Start by tracking how many new riders reach 50 orders after their first delivery, then extend the measure to 100 orders as later stages roll out. Track first-week offer achievement alongside retention, with incentive spend as the guardrail.

Two questions remain open: when to communicate potential hourly earnings, and whether riders need an intermediate step before they see the full set of offers and conditions.
