01 / THE CHALLENGE

Signing up was easy.
Knowing what came next wasn’t.

At the initial funnel snapshot, only about 1 in 9 sign-ups completed onboarding.

Riders had to choose a vehicle and store, submit documents, and wait for verification. Unclear requirements and limited visibility into what happened next created confusion at crucial moments.

The question became: how might we help riders feel confident enough to take the next step?
02 / LISTENING FIRST

The friction wasn’t just in the number of steps.

I mapped the journey and spoke with riders who had recently dropped off or completed onboarding. A discussion guide kept the conversations focused while leaving room for their stories.

DESIGNING FOR THE FIRST TOUCHPOINT

Make the right app easier to recognise.

The consumer app and the old delivery-partner app both used yellow Blinkit icons, making them easy to confuse. I explored a clearer partner identity using colour, a prominent “Delivery partner” label, and rider illustrations, while keeping the Blinkit brand recognisable.

Distinct enough to find. Familiar enough to trust.

The first change was the app icon: riders were confusing the partner app with the consumer app. The intent was to keep it simple, clearly signal “delivery partner,” and bring inclusivity into the illustrations.

Exploring black & green

I explored a dark theme using the rider app’s black and green palette for brand consistency and a clearer distinction from the consumer app.

Trying the familiar yellow

I also explored yellow versions. Although familiar to Blinkit, they risked adding to the same confusion between the consumer and partner apps.

02

Ask the right question at the right time.

Choosing a vehicle before a store helps narrow the options to relevant stores. Rather than packing more into one screen, the sequence gives each decision room to breathe.

04 / KEEPING IT CONTEXTUAL

The right help. At the right moment.

Help already existed in the flow. It just wasn’t being used. The old store-selection screen led with a “How to select store?” video with a consistently low click-through rate. It occupied the top of the page while the actual decision—which store to join—sat below the fold.

Support that knows where you are.

Help content changes based on the rider’s current step. A rider stuck at store selection sees store-specific FAQs instead of a generic list. A walkthrough video and direct call support sit alongside, accessible through a floating Help button.

Store cards built around real questions.

Riders were choosing between cards that looked identical. The old list led with a full postal address and little a rider could act on—two entries could both read “Super Store - Gurgaon Sector 53” with no easy way to tell them apart.

The card is rebuilt around the questions riders actually asked: distance from them, a landmark instead of an address, and the store’s brand, order volume, and joining bonus surfaced as tags. A ★ Best store marker highlights a recommended store. Stores that aren’t hiring stay on the list with a Notify me option rather than vanishing.

THE EXPLORATIONS

Making the useful details stand out.

I explored store-type labels, street view, order-volume tags, and different ways to present fees and bonuses. Too many badges made the cards cluttered; a strong green city bar competed with the store choice; and some card treatments didn’t feel selectable. These iterations helped simplify the hierarchy around the rider’s decision.

05 / THE DESIGNS

Designing for clarity.

Document submission was where riders dropped off hardest. The old flow prescribed a single path, hid the alternatives behind a “Skip”, and then asked riders to type out what the document already said.

In the new flow: Every accepted document is shown upfront as a choice, with Aadhaar marked as recommended rather than required. The rider picks one and knows what they’ve picked.

06 / THE DESIGNS

Removing the guesswork.

Riders didn’t know what a usable photo looked like, and couldn’t tell whether theirs had worked until it was already submitted. The step now opens with the rules — full face in frame, no mask or helmet — and the camera reports what it sees as it sees it.

07 / THE DESIGNS

Helping riders make informed decisions.

The last step asks riders for money — it’s where trust is either earned or lost. Every number that affects them is on the screen before they commit: what they pay now, what comes out of their earnings later, and what they get in return.

Referral code

Riders who hadn’t used a referral code are asked before they pay, and the discount lands on the total they’re looking at — ₹100 off, reflected in the plan they choose.

08 / LEARNING & REFINING

Feedback and reflection.

Through rider testing

Leading with vehicle selection let riders move straight through. Leading with store selection slowed them down: choosing a store took a lot of decision time and lowered their confidence.

Through development

The rest came out of building it: a skeleton loader to carry the wait between states, store pin icons redrawn for dark mode, store logos scaled down after they read too heavy in the list, and thumbnails designed for the help videos, which had shipped without them.
