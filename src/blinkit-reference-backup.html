Why we started redesigning?

Confusion, Delays, and Trust Gaps in Onboarding

Blinkit’s delivery partner onboarding involved multiple steps, from vehicle and store selection to document submission and verification.


Despite a straightforward flow, confusion around document requirements and unclear post-submission steps led to significant drop-offs. Only 1 in 9 users who signed up completed onboarding, which was quite sad for the product tbh.

How I tackled this problem?

Step 1: Mapping out the current onboarding

This was the first project i got when i joined the org, knowing the journey end to end was difficult let alone understanding riders, before going to riders i studied the flow end to end and put my fresh perspective on the notes

Step 2: Listening before designing

We spent our time with rider calling, we called two types of riders- Riders who just dropped off and rider who were recently onboarded

Research objective

Identify the root causes of delivery partner drop-offs during onboarding.

Created a questionnaire doc

These questions weren't followed to the tee but it gave me a basic idea on how to talk to the user and in which manner i should hgo about it, the questions were designed for riders who were recently onboarded and rider who were dropped off.

The challenge wasn't simply the number of steps in the onboarding flow. It was the uncertainty and friction riders encountered while moving through them.

Designing a More Logical Onboarding Flow

Although auto-selecting the city was already decided, we debated where it should sit in the redesigned journey. The product team wanted to combine it with store selection, but that page already needed to display store type, distance, accepted vehicle types, onboarding fees, and a map. Adding city selection would clutter the experience. Asking for vehicle type first also allowed us to filter relevant stores, creating a more logical flow. We moved ahead with the proposed journey.
